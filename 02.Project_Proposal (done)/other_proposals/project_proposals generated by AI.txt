Here are three distinct directions for a paper, ranging from standard statistical analysis to predictive machine learning.

The Multivariate Gait Data from the UCI Repository paired with the methodology from the paper "Smoothing spline analysis of variance models: A new tool for the analysis of cyclic biomechanical data" by Helwig & Shorter (2016) provides an excellent foundation for a project.



Paper Topics & Research Questions


Option 1: The Impact of Unilateral Bracing on Bilateral Symmetry (Recommended)

• Core Concept: When a knee or ankle is braced, how does that restriction propagate through the body? Does bracing the right knee cause a compensatory alteration in the unbraced left hip angle?
• Research Question: How do knee and ankle braces affect the dynamic symmetry and kinematic coupling between ipsilateral (same-side) and contralateral (opposite-side) lower limb joints across a complete gait cycle?
• Analysis Focus: Use the SSANOVA framework to extract the Contrast Cycle (CC) between the unbraced baseline and braced conditions. Specifically evaluate whether the "regions of difference" manifest in unbraced joints (compensatory mechanisms).

Option 2: Kinematic Adaptation Over Consecutive Cycles

• Core Concept: The dataset captures 10 consecutive gait cycles. Subjects might walk differently during their first braced cycle compared to their tenth as they learn to adjust to the brace constraint.
• Research Question: Do healthy individuals demonstrate immediate or progressive kinematic adaptation when introduced to lower-limb mechanical restrictions?
• Analysis Focus: Track the sequence of the 10 gait cycles. You can model "Gait Cycle Number" (1 to 10) as a continuous interacting covariate in your spline model to determine if the Contrast Cycle curve shifts or flattens over time.

Option 3: Classification of Joint Restrictions Using Functional Data Features

• Core Concept: Instead of just examining curves, build a predictive model. If you are provided with raw time-series joint angles, can a machine learning model correctly classify if a person is restricted by a knee brace, an ankle brace, or completely unbraced?
• Research Question: Can functional principal component scores derived from joint angle time-series reliably classify specific joint restrictions in healthy populations?
• Analysis Focus: Run Functional Principal Component Analysis (FPCA) or fit smoothing splines to reduce the time-series down to key basis function coefficients. Use those coefficients as inputs into a standard classifier (e.g., Random Forest or Support Vector Machine).
